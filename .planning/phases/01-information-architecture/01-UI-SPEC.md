## Section Taxonomy (IA-01, IA-02, IA-03)

Ground truth is the shipped s51 "Ledger" accordion (`src/form/Form.tsx`), not the stale `canvas-first-editor-design.md` plain-form description — per `STATE.md` and `INGEST-CONFLICTS.md`. Five sections, confirmed both in the section-nav labels (`Form.tsx:1459-1465`) and in `data-form-section` DOM attributes:

| Section | Data-form-section | One-sentence purpose | Belongs here | Does NOT belong here |
|---------|--------------------|----------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| Client | `client` | Who this map is for. | Household/plan identity fields (title, year). | Any dollar figure. |
| Income | `income` | Money coming in, and the after-tax total the map is built from. | Income sources (label, amount, mo/yr period), After-Tax Income total. | Account balances; the monthly need number. |
| Accounts | `accounts` | Where money currently sits. | One accordion card per account (name, value, account type, supporting note), plus each account's nested Positions and Sub-accounts (see below). | Income sources; the monthly need number. |
| Need | `need` | The red number the household must cover each month. | Monthly account withdrawal (as-needed amount), monthly need total. | See Fine print finding below — currently misplaced here. |
| Notes | `notes` | Free-text context that isn't a number. | Freeform textarea notes. | Anything with a dollar value. |

**Verified against shipped code (2026-08-09, re-run for 01-02):** `grep -o 'data-form-section="[a-z]*"' src/form/Form.tsx | sort -u` returns exactly 5 values — `accounts`, `client`, `income`, `need`, `notes` — matching this table, and `sectionLabels` (`Form.tsx:1490-1496`) maps the same five keys to `Client`/`Income`/`Accounts`/`Need`/`Notes`.

This taxonomy is already correct and unambiguous for 4 of 5 sections. The exceptions are the two concrete overlaps below — these are the actual content of IA-02/IA-03 for this phase, not the section list itself.

### Finding 1 (IA-02) — Fine print is filed under the wrong section

**What's shipped:** `FinePrintSection` (Gross/Net footnote line items) renders physically inside `NeedSection`'s JSX (`Form.tsx:523`) and is included in the `need` section's filter-match blob (`Form.tsx:1475-1481`). It has no separate nav entry — only 5 buttons exist in `data-form-sections`.

**Why it's a real overlap, not just nesting:** Fine print's fields are `Gross` / `Net` — a breakdown of an income figure ("The after-tax amount appears in green," `Form.tsx:1229`). That is income-shaped content, physically filed under the section whose one-sentence purpose is "the monthly number the household must cover." A user reading "Need" and finding gross/net income footnotes there is exactly the "unclear what lives where" complaint.

**Recommendation (default, pending Cyril confirmation):** Move Fine print's home to the Income section — it describes an income figure, so it belongs where income figures live. Concretely: render `FinePrintSection` inside `IncomeSection` instead of `NeedSection`, and fold its content into the `income` filter-match blob instead of `need`'s. Nav stays 5 buttons (Fine print is not a top-level destination, same as today) — only its *parent* changes.

**Alternative not recommended:** Promoting Fine print to a 6th top-level nav item. Rejected by default because its content volume (a handful of Gross/Net lines) doesn't warrant a standalone destination, and a 6th button pushes the nav past the "5 sections, no more" mental model IA-02 is establishing.

**Status: PENDING — needs Cyril's one-word confirmation** (same pattern as the two other open rulings this milestone carries). Plan-phase should treat "move Fine print into Income" as the default unless overridden.

### Finding 2 (IA-03) — Positions and Sub-accounts read as duplicates, but aren't

**What's shipped:** Every account card unconditionally renders two nested groups, `PositionRows` (`Position { label, value }`) and `SubAccountRows` (`SubAccount { label, caption?, value }`) — both use the generic field labels "Label" and "Value," both add via a "+ Add {position/sub-account}" button, and both appear on every account regardless of type. Nothing in the UI communicates why there are two near-identical nested-item groups.

**What they actually are (per `src/model/types.ts`):** Positions are a flat value breakdown *within* the account's own total (e.g. splitting one account by holding/carrier). Sub-accounts are a distinct earmarked pool *carved out of* the account, rendered on the map as its own inset shape (model comment: "nested earmarked pool drawn as an inset cylinder — e.g. RMD short-term funds"). These are structurally siblings but semantically different: one is "what this total is made of," the other is "a separate thing living inside this account."

**Recommendation (default, pending Cyril confirmation):** Keep both nested inside Accounts (that placement is correct — neither belongs as a sibling top-level section). Fix the labeling so the distinction is visible without reading code:
- Positions group subhead gets one line of help text: *"Break this account's total into holdings — e.g. by fund or carrier."*
- Sub-accounts group subhead gets one line of help text: *"Carve out a named pool inside this account — e.g. RMD short-term funds. It gets its own shape on the map."*

This is a copy-only fix (no layout change), so it's in-scope for Phase 1 even though field-level visual craft is Phase 3's job — see Copywriting Contract below for exact strings.

**Explicitly out of scope for Phase 1:** whether Positions/Sub-accounts should be conditionally hidden per account type (e.g. hide Positions on a checking account). That's a visibility/behavior question, not a naming question — defer to Phase 2 (Flow) or Phase 3 if it resurfaces.

**Status: PENDING — needs Cyril's one-word confirmation** on the two help-text strings (or his own alternative wording — he knows the advisor vocabulary better than a generic label would).

### Non-issues confirmed (checked, not overlaps)

- **"Supporting note" appears at both the Account level and the Sub-account level.** Same label, different nesting depth — this is the same concept (a free-text caption) reused consistently, not a competing definition. No change needed.
- **After-Tax Income (Income section) vs. Monthly need (Need section).** Different numbers, different sections, no shared vocabulary collision.

## Addendum — ambiguity sweep, 2026-08-08

Commissioned by Cyril after Finding 2: *"make sure there's no other confusing crap like this."* A dedicated read-only sweep of `Form.tsx`, `types.ts`, `book.ts`, `Wizard.tsx`, `EditorPanels.tsx`, `EditorRail.tsx` hunted for the same problem class — UI that under-communicates a distinction that is real in the model or in behavior. Three found. `Wizard.tsx`, `EditorPanels.tsx`, `EditorRail.tsx` are clean; they only re-host Form's sections.

### Finding 3 (HIGH) — Three fields print on the exported map but cannot be edited anywhere

**Verified 2026-08-08 by direct grep, not inferred.**

| Field | Defined | Prints via | Occurrences in `Form.tsx` | In `Wizard.tsx` |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| `Account.valueTag` | `src/model/types.ts:39` | `src/export/export.ts:37` | **0** | 0 |
| `MoneyMapData.needTag` | `src/model/types.ts:203` | `src/export/export.ts:41` | 1 — search index only (`Form.tsx:1478`) | 0 |
| `IncomeSource.qualifier` | `src/model/types.ts:56` | `src/export/export.ts:48` | **0** | 0 |

Sample data ships `qualifier: "Gross"`, so these render in practice. The tell: `src/form/Form.tsx:494` opens `<div className="value-tag-fields need-fields">` — a wrapper named for the tag field — containing only two `MoneyField`s and no tag control.

**Why it matters more than a naming nit:** text appears on the finished client-facing artifact that the advisor has no way to change. `needTag` is searchable but not editable — the filter will surface a field the user then cannot find. The only route to change these today is hand-editing the JSON book file.

**Recommendation:** expose all three as editable fields in their owning sections (valueTag on the account card, needTag in Need, qualifier on the income source row). This is the highest-value item in Phase 1 — it converts an invisible constraint into a visible control.

**Alternative if editing is not wanted:** remove them from export so nothing prints that cannot be controlled. Not recommended — sample data suggests they carry real meaning.

### Finding 4 (MEDIUM) — "After-tax" names three unrelated things

- Household income total: `label="After-Tax Income"` (`Form.tsx:640`, `types.ts:200`)
- Account-type option: `{ value: afterTax, label: "After-tax" }` (`book.ts:58`, select at `Form.tsx:914-931`)
- One Fine-print row's withholding figure: help text "The after-tax amount appears in green" (`Form.tsx:1229`) describing the per-row `Net` field (`Form.tsx:1206`, `types.ts:63` "after withholding")

A household total, an account category, and one row's withholding figure share a word by accident. An advisor reasonably assumes they reconcile; they do not. **Recommendation:** rename two of the three. Cyril's advisor vocabulary should pick which keeps the term.

### Finding 5 (LOW) — Panel name and printed name disagree

`label="Monthly account withdrawal"` (`Form.tsx:503-504`, bound to `asNeededAmount`) prints on the map as a chip reading **"As needed"** (`render/MapSvg.tsx:2045`; accessible label at 2002-2003 reads "Monthly income drawn as needed"). The field name describes the mechanism; the map shows the client-facing phrase. Someone hunting Data for "As needed" will not find it. **Recommendation:** align the panel label to the printed phrase.

### Finding 6 — Should Fine print exist at all?

Cyril asked: *"idk if we need fine print at all?"* and *"idk how much the money map generator should be focusing on gross vs net."*

**Investigated: keep it.** Fine print is not a form field — it is a map element with **172 references across 12 files**. It lays out (`src/layout/layout.ts`, `footnoteLineLayouts`), renders (`src/render/MapSvg.tsx`), is selectable in the inspector (`src/render/MapInspector.tsx:157-159`), supports text overrides (`MapSvg.tsx:435-437`), and exports. Deleting it is a product amputation, not a cleanup.

The answerable version of the question is about **emphasis, not existence** — how prominently the tool should push a gross/net breakdown. That is a Phase 2 (Flow) decision. Phase 1 only moves its editor into Income.

**Consequence for decision 3:** the Fine-print empty state is deferred to Phase 2 along with the emphasis question, rather than being added now.

## Addendum — items routed to later phases

Raised by Cyril during Phase 1 review; recorded here so they are not lost, but deliberately NOT Phase 1 scope.

### → Phase 2 (Flow): the generation payoff moment

Cyril: *"there should be some sort of 'generation' payoff/UI/UX that makes the user feel the value of the product... maybe when saving?? BUT idw it to feel like ceremony when they are used to powerpoint."*

**Recommendation: put it at export, not save.** Save is frequent and incidental; a moment there becomes the ceremony he is trying to avoid. Export is where the artifact becomes real and is infrequent enough to afford a beat — and it adds no click, because the user is already there. Seam exists: `exportPng` / `exportSvg` / `exportPdf` in `src/export/export.ts:298-318`.

**Form:** not a modal, not confetti — anything requiring dismissal *is* ceremony. The payoff should be the artifact reveal: the map briefly composes and settles into its final exported state, then the file lands. CSS-only, no dismissal, no added step. PowerPoint offers no equivalent moment, so a restrained one reads as craft rather than as a gate.

Also belongs in Phase 2: the gross/net emphasis question from Finding 6.

### → Phase 3 (Visual Craft): autocomplete suggestion styling

Cyril: *"the auto fill feature or whatever, the suggestions are all in like chunky bold font? idk mad ugly."*

**Diagnosed:** `src/styles/app.css:1517-1520` sets `.autocomplete-option strong { color: #0e654a; font-weight: 750; }` on the matched substring (`src/ui/Autocomplete.tsx:133-138`). Two defects — `750` is heavier than normal bold and reads as a slab at 13px, and the highlight fires **two** signals (weight *and* colour) where one suffices. Compounded by a `border-top` hairline between every option (`app.css:1509`), producing ruled rows of heavy green text.

**Fix:** one signal only. Either the green at normal weight, or ~600 weight with no colour shift. Reconsider the per-option rule.
