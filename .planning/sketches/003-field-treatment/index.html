Where a ledger can actually fail

A resting screenshot hides the whole risk. Rules carry less “I am editable” signal than boxes, which is the one thing here the research actually contests. Every state below is forced on simultaneously, so nothing hides behind a hover. Two rows changed after review: Empty is now genuinely empty rather than the word “Optional” faking a placeholder, because an empty field is the harder test of whether a ledger field reads as a field at all. Long text

Measured, not asserted

Contrast — computed, WCAG 2.2 AA
Hairline #dde1dc on paper 1.2875 : 1. Fails SC 1.4.11's 3:1. A hairline can separate rows; it can never be the thing that says “this is an input.” This is why B and C do not simply delete the border.
Field rule #868e89 on paper 3.27 : 1. Passes, with margin over the 3.014:1 threshold. Used for every editable boundary in B and C.
Focus #1e7a4a on paper 5.19 : 1. Passes. Focus carries three simultaneous cues: the rule doubles to 2px, the ground washes green, and the caption darkens from muted to ink.
Error #c03a2d on paper 5.26 : 1. Passes the 4.5:1 text bar. Never colour alone: the word “Not a number” carries it, per PRODUCT.md:39.
SC 2.4.13 Focus Appearance Deliberately not targeted, because it is AAA and the bar is AA. Meeting it would force a 6px bottom line on a 32px control, which would wreck all three.
Width — does the inline caption survive 380px
Tightest container 380 − 2 panel borders − 28 content padding − 2 detail border − 28 body padding − 20 card chrome = 300px, inside the sub-account carve-out.
Longest caption “Sub-account value” in bundled Public Sans at 12px / 600 / .02em = 109.96px. Plus 10px gap plus a 116px money column = 235.96px. 64px spare.
Breaking point Sentence case breaks below a 298px panel. The panel is pinned at 380px (app.css:2484), so C fits, but with 64px of headroom rather than comfort.
If captions went uppercase Breaks below 336px. Headroom drops to ~44px. One more reason the case ruling below matters.
C truncates long text, and that is a real cost Found in the render check, kept rather than patched because it is evidence. In column C the "Supporting note" value hard-clips mid-word: "~50% Equities / ~50% Fixed Incom". C's inline layout leaves roughly 210px for a value that needs about 210px, whereas A and B give it the full 352px. Inputs clip without an ellipsis by design and the text scrolls once focused, so nothing is lost, but at rest it reads as damage. This is the strongest practical argument against C and it only appears because the states were rendered rather than described.
200% zoom CSS pixels scale together and the app pins a 1000px shell, preserving the 380px panel by forcing page scroll. C's inline row is also shorter than a stacked one, which relieves the exact vertical starvation Move 1 was written to prevent.
What the research says, including where it argues against B
Google / Material, ~600 participants Two studies (158 + 45 pilot, then 400), across simple, complex and realistic task-based forms. They deprecated the bare underline field. Three measured failures: “some users didn't know that they could interact with and click on the text field”; “the line affordance was not clear to some users. The line was confused with a divider”; “the label and input were confused with body text, especially in dense compositions.”
Why that lands hard here The second failure is aimed at this panel specifically. It uses hairline dividers between rows in the same 380px column as its field rules. The third one lands too: a 380px column with section headers, help text and captions is a dense composition by their definition.
What replaced it, and why B survives Material's answer was not only the outlined box. It was the filled field: a tinted ground plus a bottom edge. That is what a ledger physically is, a ruled writing area on tinted paper. B originally had the rule and only a 3% ghost of a fill, which put it on the deprecated side. The fill is now 6%, and it is the affordance doing the work; the rule is the WCAG boundary.
Baymard Read directly rather than via summary, because the search result misattributed a boundary recommendation to them. They make none. They describe a container as optionally having “a fill and a stroke, either around the entire container, or just on the bottom edge”. A bottom-edge stroke is explicitly a legitimate container. Neutral, not supporting.
The honest state of it No study found here tests a paper-document financial tool specifically, so the Material result is the closest good evidence and it is a caution, not a veto. The reviewer's sharpest line stands on its own: B risks reading as less chrome, not more character. That is a design judgment, and it is Cyril's.
